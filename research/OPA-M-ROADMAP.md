# OPA-M · Open Photonic Memory Interconnect

Updated 2026-11-24. OPA-M studies a digital transport path between electronic memory and an electronic AI accelerator, with a later interface to the wider Open Photonic Accelerator ecosystem. The long-term deliverable is an open, fabricable and experimentally verifiable interconnect. The [main OPA roadmap](../PHOTONIC_RESEARCH_ROADMAP.md) retains its current circuit, component-library and passive test-chip priorities.

## Scope and hypothesis

Parallel electronic memory channels, wavelength-division multiplexing (WDM) and locality-aware inference software may increase usable memory bandwidth, reduce energy per output token and improve access to larger AI models. Each part of that hypothesis can fail independently. Optical transport leaves limits in the SSD, DRAM, controllers, accelerator ingress/cache hierarchy and compute intact. Extra laser and receiver power, endpoint circuitry, packaging, cooling, process access and software effort can make the design less accessible than an electrical solution.

The target data path is electronic memory and controller → framing/serializer and driver → modulator/MUX → shared waveguide or fiber → DEMUX/detector/TIA and receiver → integrity check, buffers and accelerator ingress. Commands, flow control and writes require their own capacity and latency budget. An optical link carries digital data. The electronic memory stores it. The separate optical delay-line topic in the main roadmap uses the provisional ID OPA-DELAY-001. OPA-MEM-001 remains the registered Chip 12 educational baseline and retains its artifacts.

For each workload compare **(A)** the strongest relevant accessible electrical baseline, **(B)** an optical transport substitution with memory, accelerator, workload and payload rate matched, and **(C)** a wider optical link with the same endpoints and explicit ceilings. Evaluate additional memory capacity, different software schedules or repackaging as separate interventions. Include GPU-resident/local memory and, where practical, host DDR plus PCIe, unified memory, electrical CXL or FPGA-to-FPGA links as appropriate to the reach and protocol. Compare latency, throughput and energy at matched checkpoint, quantization, output quality, context, batch, data integrity, reach and accounting boundary. Report price, estimated build and packaging cost, availability and access requirements as separate columns once sourced. Measured power and the compute cost of the browser simulation have separate meanings.

## Phases, dependencies and verification gates

Only OPA-M-001 is active. Its initial numerical study is implemented and reproducible. Measurement calibration is open. OPA-M-002–008 are planned and have no execution entries in the experiment registry.

Facility leads are [Myfab labs](https://myfab.se/labs/), a process-matched MPW and an academic packaging/RF test partner. Confirm eligibility, training, calibration access, quotations, IP terms and funding independently. A shared facility or research partner is a planning option. ## Starting evidence and calibration

The [OPA-M-001 methods and literature review](OPA-M-001.md) records the Cornell/Princeton/NYU HBM study and distinguishes published simulations, company disclosures, external hardware demonstrations and OPA calculations. That study motivates independently limiting accelerator ingress. This track has no physical measurements; work on bank hardware, optical design, integration and fabrication remains planned.

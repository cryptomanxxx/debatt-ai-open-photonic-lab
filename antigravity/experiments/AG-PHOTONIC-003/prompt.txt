AG-PHOTONIC-003 — Evidence-driven architecture optimization for classical photonic AI TRAINING.
PRIMARY OPTIMIZATION OBJECTIVE (mandatory): maximize sustained useful training compute throughput divided by total electrical system power, eta = useful MAC/s / W = useful MAC/J (report TOPS/W with explicit 2 operations per MAC convention). Equivalently minimize joules per useful MAC for the same workload. Count only useful completed forward, backward and weight-gradient operations, not idle lanes, redundant operations or theoretical peak capacity. Include all laser/comb, amplifier, phase control, converters, memory, electronic accumulation, optimizer, control, calibration and cooling power; amortize setup and idle power over the actual training workload. Require matched model quality, convergence, workload, batch size and precision/error constraints; report minimum throughput and latency requirements as explicit constraints or sweep them if unspecified. Also measure modeled total joules and time to a common validation-quality target, including failed convergence; never rank a nonconvergent design as optimal. Report peak and sustained throughput separately, chip-only and full-system efficiency separately, and compare electronic baseline at the same boundary. Rank feasible candidates by primary efficiency, with area, latency, absolute throughput and robustness as secondary Pareto metrics; report sensitivity and ranking reversals. Provide executed numerical evidence for every ranking and do not claim a universal optimum.
MANDATORY: Every conclusion about an optimal architecture MUST be supported by actual executed calculations and simulations, with numerical results, reproducible code, assumptions, uncertainty and comparison against alternatives. Never declare a global optimum from a finite sweep. If evidence is insufficient, report no established optimum. Negative results are welcome. Do not assume photonics wins.
Design a frequency-comb-powered WDM accelerator. Explicitly distinguish pump lasers, nonlinear microring frequency comb generators, weight rings and MZIs. Sweep 1,2,4,8 pumps and combs (including feasible shared-pump configurations), 8,16,32,64 usable comb lines, central vs distributed sources, mesh sizes 4,8,16,32,64,128,256, and 1,2,4,8,16,32 tiles. Compare MZI, MRR weight banks and hybrid designs; include independent laser array control. Reject physically infeasible combinations explicitly. Use staged screening then detailed simulation of Pareto candidates rather than exhaustive expensive electromagnetic simulations.
Model comb threshold, FSR, dispersion, usable bandwidth, line power nonuniformity, pump-to-comb efficiency, pump rejection, coupling, routing, splitting, mux/demux, propagation, detector noise, crosstalk, wavelength-dependent MZI phase and loss, thermal drift, and optional optical amplification including ASE, saturation and electrical energy. Enforce power conservation and count pump power only once. Specify exact component counts and optical topology for each finalist, including lasers, comb rings, weight rings, MZIs, phase shifters, modulators, detectors, converters and waveguides. Distinguish WDM batch parallelism (same matrix) from independent wavelength-specific weights; prove mapping and count useful MACs without double counting. For general matrices include SVD two meshes and singular-value scaling or justified alternative; include signed values, coherent encoding and detection requirements.
Compare thermo-optic, silicon carrier-depletion, Pockels TFLN/BTO, nonvolatile PCM and hybrid phase control; include static hold power, switching energy, voltage, capacitance, insertion loss, settling, footprint, thermal crosstalk, calibration, endurance and material integration feasibility. Distinguish fast data modulation from weight programming and slow resonance stabilization. Sweep 4,6,8,12-bit effective precision and update cadence. Cite verifiable device parameter sources if available; otherwise label assumptions and use broad uncertainty ranges. No invented citations.

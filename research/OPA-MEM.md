# OPA-MEM-001 · Photonic Memory Interconnect

Evidence level: deterministic analytical teaching model. The public experiment compares PCIe and a conceptual WDM link feeding the **same electronic accelerator** from the same storage and memory tiers. It uses a batch of one autoregressive request after prefill. It neither runs a language model nor measures optical hardware. Source code and test cases are in [simulations/interconnect](../simulations/interconnect).

## Equations and assumptions

Throughout, 1 GB = 10⁹ bytes, 1 Gb = 10⁹ bits and 1 pJ = 10⁻¹² J. Times are seconds. Energy represents an illustrative electrical budget for decoding the requested output tokens. Constants are exported with every run.

### Checkpoint, staging and traffic

- Let `P` be billions of total parameters, `b` the storage bits per weight, `L` the layer count, `T` the output tokens, and `S` the existing context tokens. The total weight inventory is `W = P·b/8 GB`. The active fraction `a` is 1 for dense and the chosen percentage for MoE, including shared parameters. Active parameters are `P·a` and active bytes are `W·a`.
- For `H` KV heads per layer, each generated or context token contributes `K = 2·L·H·128·2/10⁹ GB`. Factors of two account for keys/values and bytes per cached FP16 value. Peak KV capacity is `K·(S+T)`. Mean KV traffic per decode step is `K·(S+(T−1)/2+1)`, one read of prior entries plus one write. KV cache is assumed local to the accelerator.
- One representative staged layer occupies `W·f/L`, where the miss-read fraction `f = min(1,a·m)` and `m` is the MoE overfetch multiplier (1 for dense). Layer weights are treated as uniform-sized. Buffers and scratch are simplified and never spilled. VRAM reserves peak KV plus 0.75 GB and RAM reserves 1 GB.
- In warm mode, pin the full checkpoint in VRAM if it fits. Otherwise fill the remaining VRAM with pinned weights after reserving one staged layer. Fill RAM with the remaining weights when they all fit after its reserve. Otherwise warm mode caches as much as fits after one layer buffer, and streaming mode assumes no partial cache. The tiers are exclusive. Streaming mode bypasses static VRAM residency. If a tier needs staging and lacks a representative buffer, results are blocked.
- Let `G` and `R` be pinned weights in VRAM and RAM. Expected external transfer is `Wlink = (W−G)·f` and SSD read is `Wssd = (W−G−R)·f` per token. This models uniformly distributed hits with overfetch applied to misses, between active-only loads and the full uncached inventory. Temporal locality, expert skew, cold cache, disk page granularity and prefetch policy can change I/O markedly. Host DRAM moves `Wlink+Wssd` (including the write from SSD); local VRAM weight reads are modeled as `W·a` plus the separate KV path. The external link writes weights to the accelerator staging memory. The energy budget's local memory term covers compute-side reads and KV read/write; ingress write energy is not modeled separately.

### Link ceilings and optical feasibility

- For PCIe generations 1–6 the line rates are 2.5, 5, 8, 16, 32 and 64 GT/s. Raw one-way ceiling `Braw = lanes·lineRate/8 GB/s`. Encoding factors are 0.8 for 8b/10b, 128/130 for Gen3–5 and 1 for Gen6 before FLIT/FEC/protocol overhead. Payload bandwidth is raw × encoding × 0.85. The constant payload factor is educational and lumps framing, overhead and other transport inefficiency into one estimate.
- For `N` wavelengths and NRZ line rate `q` Gb/s per wavelength, `Braw = N·q/8 GB/s`. Payload is 85% of raw when the three screens below pass. For centered equally spaced frequencies `νᵢ = c/(1550 nm) + (i−(N−1)/2)·Δν` and `λᵢ = c/νᵢ`, with `i = 0…N−1`. Frequency spacing is exact in the model. Wavelength spacing varies slightly because wavelength is reciprocal to frequency.
- Spectrum screens: for multiple channels require `Δν ≥ 1.25·q` in GHz under a simplified NRZ guard factor. Require occupied span `(N−1)·Δν+1.25·q` (GHz) within the user-selected usable comb span. This guard is a teaching approximation, with no filter shapes, thermal drift, noise, line-to-line power tilt or crosstalk model.
- Power screen: loss `A = insertionLoss + length_cm·loss_dB/cm`. Receive power per channel `Pᵣ = 1000·pump_mW·0.25·10^(−A/10)/N` in µW, with equal comb-line power and aggregate 25% pump-to-usable-comb conversion. Margin `M = 10·log10(Pᵣ/Pmin)` dB must be nonnegative. Receiver sensitivity is a user-provided power threshold, not a BER prediction. A failed optical path blocks decode only when weights must use that path. One-way guide propagation time is `length_m·4/c`, using group index 4 and `c=299792458 m/s`.
- The carrier branch is pump → microcomb → carrier separator → per-channel modulators. The data branch is SSD when needed → host RAM → controller → electrical driver/serializer → modulators → MUX → shared guide → DEMUX → photodetectors → electrical receiver/deserializer → accelerator. Return/control signaling, long-haul packaging and optical buffering are outside the model.

### Latency and energy

- Host path service times are `Wssd/(0.85·Bssd)`, `(Wlink+Wssd)/(0.8·Bram)`, `Wlink/Bcontroller`, and `Wlink/Bpayload`. Stages overlap ideally. Transfer time `tₓ` is their maximum, plus one optical flight per layer when using optics. Real latency can increase from fill/drain, small I/Os, DMA and cache misses. End-to-end effective transfer is `Wlink/tₓ` where data moves.
- Local weight reads and KV traffic are each divided by `0.8·Bvram`. Compute FLOPs in billions are estimated as `2·P·a + (4·L·(4H)·128·(S+(T−1)/2))/10⁹`. The first term approximates a multiply-add per active weight. The second estimates attention dot products and value accumulation with four query heads per KV head. Compute time divides this sum by peak TFLOP/s × 1000 × 0.5 utilization. These assumptions omit model-specific operators, decompression and kernels. Weight transfers, local reads, KV, compute and 0.2 ms/token of other costs are serialized relative to each other. Their sum `t` determines `1/t` tokens/s and `T·t` seconds for the generated answer. The largest modeled cost is highlighted. Prompt prefill, model download, startup, batching and overlapping compute with I/O are excluded, so the estimate is neither absolute time to first token nor an engine benchmark.
- For `D` GB per token, the run moves `8·10⁹·D·T` bits. Per-bit coefficients in pJ are illustrative: 50 for SSD reads, 15 for host memory, 12 for PCIe payload (including electronics) and 4 for local memory. Optical dynamic energy per payload bit sums the configurable modulator, photodetector, E/O and O/E line-bit terms and divides by 0.85. Defaults in that order are 0.3, 0.2, 1 and 1 pJ. Terms are mutually exclusive within this budget.
- While the optical path is used, the model keeps it biased through the entire decode window: fixed energy `(pump_mW/(1000·0.25)+0.5 W)·T·t`. The 25% wall-plug factor and 0.5 W bias account for the pump and lumped supporting static optical electronics. Link pJ/bit divides link energy (including fixed power when applicable) by run payload bits. Total modeled energy sums link, storage, host memory, local memory, `120 W·tcompute·T` incremental compute and `35 W·T·t` baseline platform. Cooling, supply losses beyond the pump, startup, standby and detailed controller power are outside this boundary. Do not compare estimates to measured hardware energy without harmonizing the scope.

## OPA research handoff

Chip 10 explores how a nonlinear microring produces and distributes carrier lines. Chip 11 carries over thresholding, loss and the cost of converting electrical signals and light. Chip 12 asks when that interface is useful to OPA at the whole-workload level. No Chip 10/11 model or PDK parameters are silently transferred into this link budget.

A future hardware hypothesis is that WDM improves decode latency or power when host-to-accelerator communication dominates and storage, memory controllers and compute can feed the added bandwidth. The reusable [component registry](../components/registry.json) and pinned [ring/modulator circuit models](../components/external/Luxtelligence/lnoi400/s_parameters/README.md) can inform component selection. They do not supply a measured comb-to-receiver digital link. Before any component design, specify payload rate and BER, endpoint power, channel count, process compatibility, thermal drift, memory-controller traffic, support for overlapping transfers and quantized checkpoint data. Compare the electrical and optical setups at matched payload, precision, workload and bit error rate. Measure cold and warm decode separately from prompt processing.
